# Ethanol/HOPG: three order regimes in one coverage-series study

**Status: literature source and kinetic table verified; spectra not digitized.**

Burke, Wolff, Edridge & Brown report TPD of ethanol on HOPG at a 98 K dosing
surface temperature with a 0.50 ± 0.01 K/s heating ramp. Figure 5 covers
exposures from 0.5 to 300 L and labels four features: monolayer α, bilayer β,
amorphous multilayer γ, and a crystalline shoulder δ at exposures ≥50 L.

The paper reports three distinct desorption-order classes from its
fixed-temperature intensity-versus-relative-coverage analysis:

| Feature | Published order | Interpretation / limitation |
|---|---:|---|
| α, monolayer | 0.98 ± 0.13 at 151 K | Near first order; 0.5–2 L traces are mostly isolated. |
| β, bilayer | 1.52 ± 0.21 | Greater than first order; cooperative interpretation. This feature overlaps α and γ at higher dose. |
| γ, multilayer | 0.08 ± 0.07 | Near zero but measurably sub-zero in the reported estimator; assigned to hydrogen-bonded multilayer desorption. Reported energy: 56.3 ± 1.7 kJ/mol. |
| δ, crystalline shoulder | No order listed | Appears at ≥50 L and adds phase/overlap complexity. |

This is a strategically strong multi-regime benchmark source: the same study
spans near-first, greater-than-first and near-zero/fractional-order behavior,
with a coverage series and overlapping features. The order estimates are not
whole-trace Polanyi–Wigner fit results; agreement would be a model check, not
exact estimator replication. The authors explicitly report that overlap
prevents reliable energy extraction for the monolayer and that the β/γ regions
are not isolated across the full coverage series.

**Source:** D. J. Burke et al., “The adsorption and desorption of ethanol ices
from a model grain surface,” *J. Chem. Phys.* 128, 104702 (2008), DOI
[10.1063/1.2888556](https://doi.org/10.1063/1.2888556). The accessible copy
identifies copyright not evaluated; do not redistribute extracted plot data
or figures without confirming reuse rights.

## Admission limit and next step

The article PDF text and visual descriptions of Fig. 5 were accessible, but the
PDF/image endpoints return 403 in this environment, so no graph pixels were
inspected or digitized. The source is a scan candidate, not a software test.
A useful first internal extraction would be Fig. 5(a), 0.5, 1 and 2 L,
checking the isolated α leading edge; then assess whether the openly accessible
source graph pixels support a defensible γ trace. Keep the high-dose crystalline
and overlapping spectra as stress cases rather than claiming a unique
component decomposition.
