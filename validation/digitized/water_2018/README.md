# Zaki et al. (2018): D2O on Fe3O4(111)

Source: [Water adsorption on the Fe3O4(111) surface: dissociation and network formation](https://doi.org/10.1039/C8CP02333F), PCCP 20, 15764–15774, Fig. 1a, PDF page 3.
Reproduced/adapted from E. Zaki and colleagues, Royal Society of Chemistry,
under [CC-BY-3.0](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/3.0/).

The purple experimental trace is selected by its visible colour. No exposure
value is assigned from the unkeyed numerical stack. The x axis is temperature
in K; the source caption gives 3 K/s. No numerical y-axis ticks are supplied,
so `signal_figure_units` is dimensionless, normalized to the visible trace peak
relative to the recorded pixel baseline. It is not an absolute desorption rate.

[Data](digitized.csv) retain 644 recovered columns and 68 blank columns.
Do not fill missing columns automatically: overlaps, steep strokes or invisible
colour prevented a unique extraction. [Source/overlay QA](extraction_check.png)
shows the selected centerline and 15 visual check markers. Calibration and
stroke/colour sensitivity are in [quality.json](quality.json) and
[provenance.json](provenance.json). The engineering temperature placement
allowance is about 0.76 K, in addition to pixel half-width 0.25 K; neither
represents experimental temperature uncertainty.

This is an adsorption/recombination and multiple-population system, not an
established single-state positive validation case. No energy fit or mechanism
identification was performed. Original source JPEG is retained unchanged;
coordinate extraction and overlay annotations are derived changes.
