# O₂/Ag(111): island-perimeter fractional-order mechanistic challenge

**Status: source-verified mechanistic lead; no digitized trace or Studio fit.**

Günther et al. combine temperature-programmed desorption with low-energy
electron microscopy (LEEM), directly tracking oxygen coverage and island
perimeters during desorption from Ag(111). Their model uses a half-order term
for smaller islands and relates the rate to island perimeter. This makes the
study unusually valuable for testing whether a single effective Polanyi–
Wigner order can describe a morphology-dependent process. It is not a clean
single-state fractional-order reference: the physical rate law includes
island geometry and coverage evolution.

**Source:** S. Günther et al., “Desorption kinetics from a surface derived from
direct imaging of the adsorbate layer,” *Nature Communications* 5, 3853 (2014),
DOI [10.1038/ncomms4853](https://doi.org/10.1038/ncomms4853).

## Admission gate

Inspect the article and supplements for the LEEM coverage/perimeter arrays,
TPD ramp and time/temperature calibration, and licensing. Prefer the coupled
LEEM/TPD arrays over figure-only digitization. A future comparison should first
reproduce the published morphology-aware rate relation, then fit the same
measured trace with the software’s single-state order family as an explicitly
misspecified baseline. Report predictive residuals and fitted effective order;
do not interpret that order as a unique mechanism. No data are redistributed
here.
