# Coronene and ovalene on graphite: fractional-order island-desorption candidates

**Status: primary-source literature lead; no trace digitized or fitted.**

Zacharia, Ulbricht and Hertel report that benzene and naphthalene on graphite
follow first-order desorption, while larger polyaromatic molecules coronene and
ovalene exhibit fractional-order kinetics attributed to stable two-dimensional
adsorbate islands up to desorption. The paper is useful because it explicitly
contrasts molecular-size regimes on the same substrate and discusses kinetic
orders alongside activation energies and prefactors.

**Source:** R. Zacharia, H. Ulbricht and T. Hertel, “Interlayer cohesive energy
of graphite from thermal desorption of polyaromatic hydrocarbons,” *Physical
Review B* 69, 155406 (2004), DOI
[10.1103/PhysRevB.69.155406](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevB.69.155406).
An author manuscript is indexed at arXiv:cond-mat/0308451; an institutional
repository copy is also indexed by the Max Planck Society archive.

## Why this is a distinct validation case

The proposed mechanism is island stability, not the hydrogen-bonding and
multilayer phase behavior in the HOPG alcohol studies. Benzene/naphthalene can
serve as within-paper first-order comparators, while coronene/ovalene test
whether a fitted effective order departs from one for larger adsorbates. The
paper reports prefactors spanning approximately 10^14–10^21 s^-1 across
methods/systems, so parameter convention and method compatibility must be
checked before comparing fitted values.
